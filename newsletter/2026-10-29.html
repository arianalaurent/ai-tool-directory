Gemini 4 Carbon, a free local-AI Mac app, and chat-based video editing

7 verified releases this week, plus our pick for running AI privately on your own computer.

Hi friend,

Here's what launched in AI this week, checked against each maker's own announcement before it went on the site. New entries stay unrated until we've reviewed them, so treat these as "worth a look," not verdicts.

New this week

  • Gemini 4 Carbon (Google, Oct 28). Google says it ranks #1 on v5.0 of the Artificial Analysis Intelligence Index and sets a new state of the art in cybersecurity and long-horizon tasks. Access starts with cyber defenders in Google's Fairwind Program, so most of us are waiting for now. Google's announcement
  • Nativ (Neywa Labs, Oct 27). An open-source Mac app for running open language, vision, audio and code models locally and offline on Apple Silicon. It suggests a model that fits your Mac and can serve a local endpoint for coding agents. No subscription, per its site. Announcement
  • Cardboard (Oct 26). Describe an edit in ChatGPT or Claude and Cardboard cuts, captions and reframes it. The Lite tier in the Mac app is free with a watermark; web plans start at $8/month, per Cardboard's pricing page. Announcement
  • Silvia MCP (Oct 26). Silvia's "AI CFO" as an MCP server, so the agents you already use can see your live financial context. Free to start, per Silvia. Only for people comfortable connecting financial accounts to an AI tool. Announcement
  • Kimi K3.1 Pro (Moonshot AI, Oct 23). An open-weight model with a 1M-token context window, aimed at full-project coding, per Moonshot. Announcement
  • Canva Craft (Oct 22). Canva's first in-house image model, built for design work and available to everyone in Canva, per Canva. Announcement
  • Ling-3.0-flash-Sante (Ant Ling, Oct 22). Open-weight healthcare model on Hugging Face (MIT license) for medical research workflows. For researchers with the hardware to run it, not for personal medical advice. Announcement

Use case of the week: run AI privately on your own computer

Best pick: Ollama (our rating 9.0, #1 in Free & Open Source). It's the easiest way to run open models locally: one-line install, a model library, and a local API other apps can talk to. Skip it if your machine is low on RAM.

  • Prefer clicking to typing commands? LM Studio (8.5) gives you a desktop app to browse, download and chat with models offline.
  • On an Apple Silicon Mac? This week's Nativ is worth a try. It's still unrated while we test it.
